# Beyond the Stopwatch: When Should Combine Tracking Change a Scouting Decision?

Receiver A ran an observed 4.54-second 40; Receiver B ran 4.52. Their sprint times are close, yet their Combine turning profiles differ. Does that extra movement information justify a different scouting evaluation?

We measure TURN from lateral acceleration across multiple drills. The illustrated speed-out paths show movement that the stopwatch cannot describe. They do not establish which receiver cuts more efficiently or will perform better in the NFL.

For 23 evaluation receivers, adding TURN to a baseline containing conventional measurables, draft information and NFL context changed the error in predicting athlete mean separation from 0.23572 to 0.23577 yards. The uncertainty interval did not support an improvement. Across all nine prescribed receiver and line comparisons, none of the primary intervals supported improved prediction.

We propose using movement differences to focus a specific film question: how does each receiver enter and exit comparable out cuts? A draft recommendation would require further performance evidence. This film-review use has not been tested in scouting.

Our evidence map separates three questions: can we measure the movement consistently, does it carry into games, and does it improve regular-season performance prediction?
