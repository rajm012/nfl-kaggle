Beyond the stopwatch¶

Combine movement to NFL decisions: an evidence map¶

NFL Big Data Bowl 2027 · University Track

Receiver A ran 4.54 seconds and B 4.52, yet their Combine turning profiles differ. Does that extra movement information justify a different scouting evaluation?

We follow the evidence through three questions: can we measure it, does it transfer, and does it add predictive value? None of nine primary prediction intervals supports an improvement. We propose using movement differences to focus film questions, with further evidence needed before changing a recommendation. This use has not been tested in scouting.

01 · What the stopwatch misses¶

The 40-yard dash captures straight-line speed. Route tracking adds the shape of movement: how a player turns, accelerates and builds speed. The selected receivers have close 40 times, but their across-drill TURN profiles are −1.78 and +2.53 development-WR SD. Both profiles use the same 13 drills; the displayed speed-out is one recorded attempt.

Figure 1. One speed-out attempt per receiver; headings describe across-drill profiles. The pair maximizes TURN contrast among 2023–24 WRs with observed 40 times within 0.03 seconds; deterministic ties and first recorded attempts. No NFL outcomes selected this extreme example. Dotted lines/crosses mark separately selected interior acceleration peaks, rather than verified cut onset; fixed speed windows are descriptive and gray shading marks derivative edges.

TURN summarises lateral acceleration through a drill. BURST summarises peak forward acceleration. A0 describes the early acceleration of the fitted 40-yard speed curve. Positions are smoothed at 10 Hz; acceleration is decomposed into signed longitudinal and lateral components. Attempts are averaged within drill, standardized within drill and averaged across available non-40 drills. Standardisation for prediction uses 2023–24 athletes only.

Lateral acceleration = speed² × curvature. At the reference peaks, B is faster (5.879 versus 5.433 yd/s) along a slightly wider local curve (curvature 0.233 versus 0.249 1/yd). A higher TURN value therefore does not establish a tighter or more efficient cut. The speed-out contributes 5.9% arithmetically to the profile gap; one attempt does not explain the composite. These quantities use the written descriptive protocol and unchanged kinematics. They do not establish individual talent, repeatability or a causal effect.

The supplied tracking covers 510 rookie-cohort players. Availability narrows as we require a usable drill, an NFL outcome and enough repeated observations. Each comparison therefore reports its own athlete count.

03 · Movement carries into games¶

Some Combine movement differences reappear in NFL tracking. Receiver TURN correlates with game turning at 0.279 [0.107, 0.445] across 98 athletes. Defensive-line BURST correlates with game burst at 0.443 [0.289, 0.584] across 115 athletes; after weight adjustment, the estimate is 0.203 [0.044, 0.376]. WR transfer after measured size/40 adjustment is 0.278 [0.092, 0.450]; this does not hold the illustrated drill speeds constant.

Figure 3. Descriptive movement transfer, with athlete-bootstrap intervals and refitted adjustment. These pooled PRE + REG estimates are separate from regular-season performance prediction. Historical full-cohort normalization differs from development reliability and training-only prediction.

Development WR cross-drill TURN consistency is 0.593 [0.373, 0.737], based on 70 receivers. Cohort cross-drill agreement does not establish either receiver's individual repeatability. The separate same-speed-out repeat estimate is r=0.012 [−0.442, 0.492], only 17 pairs, and remains highly uncertain.

Before testing whether TURN predicts a route-specific performance advantage, we required a stable SLANT/OUT-minus-GO contrast. The adjusted reliability was 0.296 [−0.257, 0.606] among 41 diagnostic receivers, below the predeclared 0.40 threshold.

05 · Does tracking improve performance prediction?¶

We trained conventional baselines on 2023–24 athletes and compared them with the same models plus tracking on 2025 athletes. Both models score identical rows; each athlete contributes equal weight to RMSE. Evaluation-player intercepts are never fitted.

None of the nine primary marginal intervals supports an RMSE gain. Every original-family simultaneous interval includes zero. This is uncertainty about added value, not evidence of equivalence.

06 · A scout’s evidence card¶

The proposed request: on comparable out routes, how does each receiver approach the break, change direction and build speed afterward? Record route depth, alignment, motion, coverage, cushion and throw timing. Define the eligible clip set before reviewing its results; retain contradictory clips and missing context. No film assessment has been performed here.

The meeting produces a film question and an evidence gap. A TURN-based grade change needs repeatable individual measurements and independently supported relevant NFL performance information beyond conventional evaluation. The current results do not supply those requirements.

Figure 6. A proposed receiver-review workflow: observe movement, ask a specific film question and record the evidence needed before changing a recommendation. Cohort agreement, movement transfer and added performance prediction remain separate.

The limits matter: small evaluation samples, prior 2025 exposure, omitted training uncertainty, selection into NFL playing time and cone testing, observational context, and incomplete opponent tracking. Supplied separation is distance to the nearest defender at throw; W1 pools routes and does not measure separation caused by an out cut. Get-off is snap-to-LOS-crossing time, rather than movement-onset latency. No individual change threshold or benefit from training TURN is established, and the workflow's decision usefulness remains untested.

They change the estimator as well as uncertainty and are explicitly sensitivities.

The preregistration stated expected signs but did not explicitly state one-sided testing. Original code used directional p-values. This audit conservatively reports two-sided p-values and Holm across the six Study 1 LMM and three Study 2 confirmatory rows separately. The three cluster sensitivities form a separately labelled Holm family. Historical directional values are saved in JSON, not promoted as new findings.

Study 2 A0 and 10-yard-split imputation/SDs now use unique sprint-tracked athletes within position, before joining to plays. Historical code weighted these moments by opportunity. Baseline Study 1 preprocessing is retained. This is a full-cohort historical audit, not held-out prediction or a pristine 2025 evaluation.

Patsy removes only rows missing required formula fields; model groups follow the actual retained indices. Receiver movement: speed, curvature and drill contributions

Step 2 — What the receiver movement difference means

Descriptive review of the existing pair, using the written measurement protocol. Rebuild with .venv/bin/python -m src.wr_movement. Values and hashes: wr_movement_metrics.json.

The plain-language explanation

TURN measures lateral acceleration: how strongly the direction of movement changes while a player is moving. A faster player on the same curved path has more lateral acceleration. At the same speed, a tighter path also has more lateral acceleration. A larger TURN value therefore does not by itself mean better or more efficient cutting.

For motion above the written speed floor, absolute lateral acceleration = speed² × absolute path curvature. This identity describes these same position-derived quantities; it is not independent validation or a causal breakdown of talent. Forty times measure a different task and do not hold these drill speeds constant.

Full-attempt percentiles, composite headings and cached kinematics were checked against the recorded values. Local summaries require 10-Hz cadence, finite values, an interior speed floor and fixed window support. Failures remain unavailable; the saved metrics record each gate. Fixed summaries

  1. Full-attempt lateral acceleration: reproduce the existing nearest-rank 90th percentile of absolute a_lat (Polars' recorded quantile(0.9) default). Report yd/s², separately from the unitless across-drill profile. Include event frame count and recorded elapsed span (last time − first time), not a claimed timed drill completion.
  2. Interior reference peak: discard the first and last three samples for new local summaries to avoid derivative edges. Require speed at least 1 yd/s and finite derived quantities. Choose the first maximum absolute lateral acceleration in the remaining frames, using time order for ties. Call it the interior lateral-acceleration peak, not detected cut onset, ideal break, or reaction time.
  3. At that peak: report elapsed time, speed (yd/s), absolute lateral acceleration (yd/s²), absolute curvature (1/yd) and approximate local radius 1/abs(curvature) (yd). Report radius unavailable if absolute curvature is at most 1e-6. Above the speed floor, abs(a_lat) = speed² × abs(curvature) is a kinematic identity, not causal attribution.
  4. Nearby speed: take the median speed in [peak−0.3 s, peak−0.1 s] and [peak+0.1 s, peak+0.3 s], using interior, finite frames above the same speed floor. Require three samples in each window. Otherwise mark the window unavailable; do not move the peak, extend the window or lower the threshold. Call these pre/post-peak windows, not validated drill-entry/exit phases. Report their difference, not an efficiency score or a validated reacceleration metric.
  5. Across-drill explanation: retain all available drill rows for each selected athlete, their attempt counts, mean raw percentile, saved reference mean/SD, standardized value and contribution z / number of available drills. Show absent drills as absent, never impute. Only decompose B−A into drill contributions if the two drill sets are identical; otherwise explain separate composites and their drill-mix limitation.

Quality gates

  • Verify saved player/event IDs, canonical speed-out label, frame counts, unique times, strictly increasing 0.1-second cadence (absolute tolerance 1e-6 s), finite coordinates and cached quantities, and sufficient interior samples. If cadence fails, local physical summaries are unavailable; do not silently resample or repair the frozen track.
  • Cached/recomputed kinematics must agree within rtol=1e-10, atol=1e-10. Reproduced historical percentiles/profile values must agree within rtol=1e-10, atol=1e-10.
  • Check the identity on the eligible interior frames and preserve all window availability failures. Do not treat algebraic reconstruction as independent measurement validation.
  • Meaningful synthetic checks cover straight motion, circular motion at known radius, speed dependence and translation/rotation invariance. These check physical behavior, not agreement with a copied implementation.
  • No new cross-athlete outcome associations, athlete rankings, reliability thresholds, route-gate changes or training-effect claims. Existing same-drill repeat results may be cited with their sample/uncertainty limits, without fitting another reliability model.
